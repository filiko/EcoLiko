How it works
How energy is estimated

Each AI model has a measured or estimated energy cost per 1,000 tokens processed — separate rates for input (prompt) and output (generation). Output tokens cost roughly 5× more because they are generated one at a time, while the prompt is processed in parallel.

That raw compute energy is multiplied by the datacenter's PUE (Power Usage Effectiveness) — the ratio of total facility power to IT equipment power. A PUE of 1.10 means 10% overhead for cooling, lighting, and power conversion. Hyperscalers like Google and Microsoft achieve 1.09–1.12; the global industry average is around 1.56.

How water is consumed

Datacenters cool their servers using water — either directly in cooling towers, or indirectly by cooling the air that cools the servers. The key metric is WUE (Water Usage Effectiveness): liters of water consumed per kWh of IT electricity.

This water is evaporated into the atmosphere, not returned to the local water supply. That distinction matters: withdrawing water from a river and returning it (slightly warmer) is very different from evaporating it — which removes it from the local watershed entirely.

On-site vs. off-site water: Companies like Google report only on-site (Scope 1) water — the cooling towers at their datacenters. But the electricity powering those datacenters also requires water at the power plant (Scope 2). Li et al. 2023 found the off-site figure adds roughly 3× more: Google's global average on-site WUE is 1.0 L/kWh; the off-site consumption adds another ~3.1 L/kWh.
The water cycle counterargument

The common objection is that evaporated water eventually returns as rain — so nothing is really "lost." This is true globally. Water is a closed planetary system.

The problem is geography and timing. Water evaporated in Phoenix, Arizona redistributes across atmospheric circulation patterns — it may fall as rain over the Pacific Ocean, or the midwest, weeks or months later. Meanwhile, Phoenix (and many other datacenter hubs) sits in one of the most water-stressed regions in the world, drawing from the Colorado River, which is already over-allocated.

Consumptive use is the term researchers use for water that leaves the local watershed. Even if it returns to the planet, it does not return to the same reservoir, lake, or aquifer. For communities that depend on those water sources, the distinction is real.

Li et al. 2023 explicitly separates water withdrawal (taken and returned) from water consumption (evaporated, not returned). The figures in EcoLiko use consumption — the more conservative and locally meaningful measure.

Uncertainty

No paper has directly measured the energy of a GPT-4 or Claude inference. Cloud providers do not disclose per-query figures. All estimates — including this app's — are inferred from GPU power specs, public API latency, and published datacenter efficiency metrics. The realistic uncertainty range is 0.3×–3× around any given number.

The methodology setting lets you explore that range: Industry figures reflect what companies self-report; Environmental figures reflect full lifecycle accounting by independent researchers.
